Before You Travel to Sri Lanka

We believe informed travellers are happy travellers. Find answers to every question below — and if something is missing, just ask us.

🛂 Visas & Entry
Most nationalities require an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) before arriving in Sri Lanka. It costs approximately USD $20–35 and can be obtained online at eta.gov.lk within minutes. Citizens of Singapore, Maldives and Seychelles are exempt. A few nationalities require a traditional visa from a Sri Lankan embassy. We will advise you specifically based on your passport.
The standard tourist ETA permits a stay of up to 30 days, extendable to 90 days by applying at the Department of Immigration in Colombo. We handle this process for our guests on longer itineraries.
Not at all. The process takes around 10 minutes online at eta.gov.lk. You will need a valid passport (6 months minimum validity), a return flight booking, and a credit/debit card. Approval is usually instant or within a few hours. We will send you a step-by-step guide when you confirm your booking.

When the southwest monsoon brings rain to Colombo and Galle (May–September), the east coast is sunny and calm. 🛡️ Safety & Health
Sri Lanka is generally a very safe destination for international travellers. The locals are famously warm and hospitable, and the country has an excellent reputation for visitor safety. Standard travel precautions apply — keep valuables secure, be mindful in busy markets, and follow your guide's advice in national parks. Our team provides a full safety briefing before every tour.
No vaccinations are legally required for entry. However, we recommend you are up to date on routine vaccinations (MMR, Tdap, flu). Hepatitis A and Typhoid vaccinations are advisable for those visiting rural areas or eating street food. Malaria prophylaxis may be considered for those spending extended time in the dry zone — consult your GP or a travel health clinic 6–8 weeks before departure.
We recommend drinking only bottled or filtered water. All our partner hotels and eco-lodges provide safe drinking water, and we supply water on all guided days. To minimise plastic use, we encourage guests to carry a reusable bottle which can be refilled from filtered sources.
💳 Payments & Money
The Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR) is the local currency. US dollars and euros are widely accepted at hotels and larger restaurants. ATMs are available in all cities and most major towns. We recommend carrying some LKR for markets, small restaurants, and tips. A budget of approximately LKR 3,000–5,000 per day for personal expenses is comfortable for most travellers.
We accept bank transfers (USD, EUR, GBP), credit/debit cards (Visa, Mastercard) via our secure payment portal, and PayPal. A 30% deposit confirms your booking, with the balance due 45 days before departure. We never charge hidden fees.

🎒 Packing List
Essentials: Lightweight, breathable clothing (cotton or moisture-wicking). Long sleeves and trousers for temple visits and wildlife safaris. A light rain jacket or poncho. Comfortable, sturdy walking shoes. Sandals for beach and temple visits (easily removed).

Health: High-SPF sunscreen, insect repellent (DEET-based for jungle areas), personal medications, and a small first aid kit.

Temples & Culture: Pack a light sarong or shawl — useful for covering shoulders and legs at religious sites. Both men and women are required to cover up at most temples.
Yes. All temples and religious sites in Sri Lanka require both men and women to cover their shoulders and knees. Many sites also require shoes to be removed. We provide guidance before each visit, and sarongs are available at the entrance of most major sites for a small fee. Wearing respectful clothing at all times in rural villages is also appreciated.
🙏 Local Etiquette
Greetings: The traditional greeting is "Ayubowan" (wishing you a long life) with palms pressed together. Shaking hands is also acceptable.

Photography: Always ask permission before photographing people, especially monks and at religious ceremonies. Never pose in front of or turn your back to Buddha statues for photographs — it is considered deeply disrespectful.

Tipping: Not mandatory but greatly appreciated. A standard tip for guides is LKR 1,000–2,000 per day; for drivers, LKR 500–1,000.
Temple interiors and statues can generally be photographed, but always look for posted signs restricting photography (common in fresco areas). Photographing monks is welcomed with permission — approach respectfully, never touch a monk, and always accept any blessing graciously. Our guides will advise on the specific etiquette at each site.

Quick Facts: Sri Lanka

  • Capital: Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte (administrative), Colombo (commercial)
  • Currency: Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR)
  • Time Zone: GMT +5:30
  • Language: Sinhala, Tamil; English widely spoken
  • Electricity: 230V / 50Hz · Type D & G plugs
  • Dialling code: +94
  • Emergency: 119 (Police) · 110 (Ambulance)

Travel Insurance

We strongly recommend comprehensive travel insurance that covers trip cancellation, medical evacuation, and health emergencies. Sri Lanka's healthcare in major cities is good, but rural areas can be far from hospitals.
