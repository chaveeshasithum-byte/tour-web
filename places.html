Across Sri Lanka

The Pearl of the Indian Ocean

An Island of a Thousand Faces

In an island no larger than Ireland, Sri Lanka packs more variety than most continents could manage. Ancient cities that predate Rome, cloud-draped mountains thick with tea, beaches of impossible colour, and jungles alive with leopards and elephants — every corner holds something that will stop you in your tracks.

Heritage & Culture

Sigiriya — The Lion Rock

The Palace in the Sky

Few places in the world carry the weight of legend quite like Sigiriya. Built in the 5th century by King Kashyapa — a ruler who chose a 200-metre granite monolith as his fortress and palace — it remains one of Asia's most remarkable architectural achievements. The ancient frescoes of golden-skinned celestial maidens, painted onto the rock face centuries before the Renaissance, still shine with extraordinary life.

The approach through the water gardens — the oldest surviving symmetrical pleasure gardens in Asia — sets a tone of quiet wonder that only intensifies as you ascend through the Lion Gate, past the mirror wall inscribed with a millennium of visitor poetry, to the summit plateau where the entire Cultural Triangle unfolds beneath your feet.

Travel Tips

Climb at sunrise (open from 7 am) to beat the heat and the crowds. Wear sturdy shoes — the final staircase is steep. Carry water and a light jacket for the summit breeze. Photography is restricted in the fresco area; respect the rules.
